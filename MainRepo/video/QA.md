# Q&A cheat sheet (6 minutes with AEP engineers)

Numbers below are from the integrated build (replay seed 8, Monte Carlo seed 42). Re-check after any tuning.

- **Where does "4 to 12 hours" come from?** Real labor hours per damage type from AEP's workbook (pole 4.8 h, fuse 1.2 h, ...) plus the workbook's adders (access +0.5, backyard +0.5, specialized handling +0.5, remote/track machines +1.3), ×4.5 for travel and nonproductive time; crews from a regional pool of 150 split by expected repair hours with at least 1 per line (169 in total once the minimum is applied, the same allocation the calibration used); 1,000 Monte Carlo runs; we show P10 to P90.
- **Is the replay realistic?** Replayed repairs finish in 0/1/2/3+ days at 34/41/17/7% vs real Feb 2025 ARCOS 39/33/18/10% (one-day repairs a bit high, 3+ days a bit low). Run `python -m app.models.calibrate_restoration`.
- **Is the outage map real?** Damage and network are real. The live feed is simulated: about 1,175 real Feb 13-18 2025 damage reports are replayed on a clock and traced through the real upstream map (`matched_upstream.txt`) to the homes each one knocks out (about 21,700 homes go out at some point, peak about 9,200 at once on Friday morning). Capacitor-bank and streetlight damage don't cause outages. AEP's real outage feed would plug into the same slot.
- **Why did Maya's estimate change?** The estimate only knows damage reported so far (A13). At 3:41 AM it said back about 8 AM to 4 PM. At 9:29 AM a second damaged spot on her line was reported, so the return time moved about 6 h later (about 1 PM to 9 PM) and she got an "estimate updated" message. Power came back at 7:16 PM, inside the updated range; the first range was early. We picked an example where an update mattered, and we show the recap on purpose. Across 200 Monte Carlo seeds the updated range always contained the restore time.
- **How often is the first estimate right?** Honest answer: on a sample of 400 outage groups from the replay, the real restore time lands inside the first P10-P90 range about 50% of the time; almost all misses are "early" because later damage hadn't been reported yet. That's exactly why we send updates. Next step: add expected not-yet-reported damage to the first estimate.
- **How good is the risk band?** Trained on Sep 2024 only, tested on Feb 2025 with history cut before the storm: AUC 0.70; the High band (top 15% of homes) held 33% of homes with mapped storm damage on their upstream equipment (2.2× its share). The learned part alone does not beat the simple "past damage on your circuit" rule; the hybrid beats both. Caveat: we chose the hybrid among 3 candidates on that same Feb test, so it may be slightly optimistic. See `app/models/RISK_MODEL.md`.
- **Why can't some homes get a band?** 48 circuits had no damage before Feb 2025, so those homes (about a third) get "We can't predict this reliably" and a link to the official outage page instead of a guess.
- **What does "switching" do?** Network assumption. On: only repairs on your own path matter. Off: you wait for every repair on your line (Maya: about +25 h).
- **Why don't more crews help Maya?** Her line already has 15 crews; her wait is the repair itself, not a queue. The app says so. Roads blocked (+5 h) and one more damaged spot (+7 h) do move it.
- **Why does one damaged wire take out a whole line?** Damage maps to the nearest transformer's first fuse or recloser (A2). Where the network map shows no fuse or recloser between a transformer and the substation, the next device up is the breaker, so the outage is line-wide. Real crews would often sectionalize; that's a limit of the replay, and the "switching" setting is our stand-in.
- **Privacy?** Salted-hash home ids, ~1 km cells with 10+ homes, no addresses or road names (map tiles capped at zoom 12), no device/circuit ids, no crew info, life-support flags never used, declared needs never stored or logged (query strings are redacted from the server log). `tests/test_d_privacy.py` scans every endpoint; an independent audit of about 3,800 requests found no leaks.
- **What if the AI makes something up?** Numbers come only from tools; every number in an LLM reply (digits or words) must appear in tool output, and any percent is rejected, or the template answer is sent instead; safety and medical questions are always answered with fixed text; emergencies (downed lines, gas or CO, someone not breathing, self-harm) get a fixed 911 or 988 reply before any model runs. Works with no LLM key at all.
- **Alerts?** Our documented rules (not AEP policy): pre-storm if Medium/High, outage confirmed, estimate moved > 3 h or 25%, restored. SMS when the phone is in low-power mode; a call offer when a medical need is declared and the outage may last over 12 h.
- **What would you need next?** AEP's live outage/AMI feed, real ETRs, weather forecasts, more regions and storms.
